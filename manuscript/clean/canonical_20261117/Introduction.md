# Introduction

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) arises from interactions among host inflammation, treatment, microbial community structure and microbial activity. Multi-omic profiling has linked IBD to coordinated shifts in taxa, functions and metabolites across longitudinal and cross-sectional cohorts [1–9], while cross-cohort studies show that these signals vary with disease phenotype, treatment history and sampling context [6,9]. Microbiome-based classifiers therefore have value only when their performance can be interpreted against the study design that generated the measurements.

Cross-cohort prediction remains technically difficult because the estimand changes when repeated visits are split at the sample level, compositional transformations are fitted outside the source training set, or target provenance and direction are pooled. Existing microbiome machine-learning workflows and IBD prediction studies address parts of this problem through study-aware splitting, compositional analysis, profiling and external validation [10–16,17–26,29]. Graph attention adds a further decision: a source-derived association graph changes the representation together with the learner, so a performance difference cannot by itself separate model-family ranking from an input-edge effect. Participant dependence, source-only preprocessing, graph construction, provenance transfer and probability error also use different fitting and uncertainty units, making their joint alignment technically demanding in heterogeneous cohorts.

We therefore developed an estimand-explicit, source-only protocol for bidirectional microbiome classifier transfer. The protocol fixes a label-free 126-feature registry and metadata boundary; fits closure, CLR transformation, prevalence filtering, feature selection and topology only on source participants; evaluates HMP2-to-PRISM and PRISM-to-HMP2 transfer; and compares No-Prior GAT with elastic net, random forest, a no-message-passing MLP and XGBoost under the same external panels. Known-truth controls validate the data-processing and evaluation procedure, whereas same-learner topology interventions quantify conditional input-edge sensitivity. Participant-cluster bootstrap, target-origin sensitivity and probability-error references are reported as separate estimands. Applied to HMP2/IBDMDB and PRISM, the framework identifies when a relational representation improves external prediction under a declared contract and when that advantage disappears.
