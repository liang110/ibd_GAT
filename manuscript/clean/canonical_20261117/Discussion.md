# Discussion

The analysis provides a practical way to test whether a relational microbiome representation adds information to an external IBD prediction task. The framework links a frozen feature registry, source-only fitting, participant-aware estimands, known-truth controls, origin-held-out transfer, topology diagnostics, capacity comparison and paired target uncertainty. In the HMP2/IBDMDB–PRISM application, the No-Prior Spearman graph improved the HMP2-to-PRISM direction, including against a capacity-matched no-message-passing MLP, while tabular references were stronger in the reverse direction and in the PRISM origin transfer.

The result is a conditional representation finding. The topology audit shows that graph construction is reproducible under the final contract, while the capacity comparator shows that the favorable forward score is not explained by generic nonlinear capacity alone. These results do not identify a unique biological mechanism: fixed-k edge construction is based on source associations, edge-set overlap is modest, and graph-ranked features have not been independently validated. The practical implication is that a relational model should be selected only after the same directional audit has been passed; a tabular model remains the appropriate choice in directions where the graph does not transport.

The input contract resolves the main comparability risks in the current study. HMP2 and PRISM are closed within the same 126-feature registry before source-fitted CLR and selection; clinical and identifier fields are removed from every branch; twelve HMP2 zero-mass samples are recorded; and the exact-name metabolite intersection is zero. Mutual-information selection, scaling and graph construction are source-training operations. PRISM discovery and validation are separated, and participant-cluster bootstrap is kept distinct from source-repeat variability. The estimand is therefore a bounded transfer question under a fixed registry.

The known-truth and repeated-participant experiments are method-validation evidence. The collapse from naive to participant-grouped AUROC demonstrates the cost of treating repeated samples as independent. Null and batch-only controls remain near chance, while signal, reversing-batch and topology-shift scenarios recover planted features under declared rules. These tests support the auditing procedure but do not establish IBD biological generalization. The two-cohort origin audit also cannot separate country, sequencing run, extraction batch, disease activity or treatment because the available manifest does not contain validated fields for those effects.

The framework contribution is methodological and evidentiary. Cross-study microbiome evaluation, compositional analysis, graph learning and confound adjustment each have established precedents [10–36]. The contribution is their integration into a contract in which every label-dependent step is source-bounded, every comparison is directional, repeated participants are handled at the participant level, and every graph result is accompanied by validity, topology and capacity controls. The machine-readable manifest, unified ledger and source registry make the numerical evidence reusable without changing the estimand.

A third independent cohort would strengthen generality, but it is outside the current two-cohort evidence boundary. Geographic or batch attribution also requires validated PRISM metadata that are not present in the current contract. Fully nested tuning and additional compositional association references would be appropriate if the manuscript were expanded into a comprehensive model-selection benchmark. They are not required for the present bounded framework claim. Metabolite ID mapping, prospective clinical validation and wet-lab testing are outside the current endpoint.

The scope is therefore clear: the framework supports a direction-specific relational gain in one transfer edge and a reproducible negative result in others. Both outcomes are informative because the information boundary, capacity-matched comparator and participant-level uncertainty make them directly auditable.



The current evidence package adds a nested source-only XGBoost comparator and a contract-frozen topology intervention. XGBoost is interpreted as a specified tabular reference: each source panel selects settings using source-only folds and refits the selected setting on that panel before external scoring. The topology result is an input-configuration contrast under the formal full-source-batch intervention, not a decomposition of the primary minibatch GAT score.
