# Abstract

Microbiome-based classification of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is increasingly used for cross-cohort prediction, yet repeated sampling, compositional scale, cohort provenance and representation choice can change the estimand. Existing microbiome machine-learning workflows provide study-aware splitting, compositional preprocessing and external validation, but they rarely place model-family ranking, directional transfer, input-edge sensitivity and probability error under one frozen information boundary; strong nonlinear tabular references also require evaluation under the same contract. We developed an estimand-explicit, source-only protocol that fixes a 126-feature microbial registry, fits all data-dependent operations on source participants, preserves participant units and evaluates both transfer directions. In HMP2/IBDMDB and PRISM, ten source fits and 5,000 paired target-participant draws gave AUROC 0.8565 for No-Prior GAT in HMP2-to-PRISM transfer, compared with 0.7665 for the no-message-passing MLP, 0.7572 for random forest, 0.7137 for elastic net and 0.6990 for XGBoost; the GAT-minus-XGBoost contrast was +0.1573 (95% interval, 0.0838–0.2310). In PRISM-to-HMP2 transfer, XGBoost (0.7028) and random forest (0.6973) exceeded GAT (0.6138), with GAT-minus-XGBoost −0.0886 (−0.1617 to −0.0169). Known-truth controls and topology interventions were treated as protocol diagnostics, with the forward Real–Rewired contrast positive in 5/10 source splits. The framework converts a nominal graph-versus-tabular comparison into auditable, direction-specific estimands for microbiome transfer.

Keywords: inflammatory bowel disease; microbiome; cross-cohort transfer; graph attention; compositional data; participant-aware uncertainty; XGBoost

# Conclusion

Under the frozen 126-feature contract and source-only participant-aware evaluation, the relational representation contributed additional information in HMP2-to-PRISM transfer, including against XGBoost and a capacity-matched no-message-passing MLP, whereas reverse transfer favoured tabular references. The framework makes this directional result auditable through source-bounded fitting, known-truth controls, topology diagnostics, participant-cluster bootstrap and a unified prediction ledger.
