# Discussion

This work establishes an auditable way to ask when a relational microbiome representation adds information to an external IBD prediction task. The framework links a frozen feature contract, source-only fitting, participant-aware estimands, known-truth controls, origin-held-out transfer, topology diagnostics, capacity control and paired uncertainty. In the worked HMP2/IBDMDB–PRISM application, that sequence converted an apparently simple classifier comparison into a directional evidence problem: the No-Prior Spearman graph improved HMP2-to-PRISM transfer, whereas elastic net and random forest were stronger in the reverse direction and the PRISM origin analysis showed no graph advantage.

The result answers the architectural paradox raised by Reviewer 1. The graph branch is useful as a tested representation and as a diagnostic of topology dependence, but the evidence does not support a universally portable graph classifier or a claim that graph-ranked taxa are conserved ecological interactions. The topology controls show that the result changes when the association rule is changed, and the capacity control shows that generic nonlinear capacity does not fully explain the forward gain while it can be preferable in the reverse direction. The practical implication is that a tabular model may be the appropriate deployment choice when the graph fails to transport; the framework earns its value by making that failure visible under a like-for-like boundary.

The input and validation contract addresses the implementation concerns raised by Reviewers 2 and 3. HMP2 and PRISM were closed in the same 126-feature space before source-fitted CLR and selection; clinical and identifier fields were removed from every branch; zero-mass samples were recorded rather than silently filled; and the exact-name shared metabolite space was zero. Mutual-information selection, scaling and model fitting were source-training operations. The PRISM discovery and validation strata were evaluated separately, and participant-cluster bootstrap was kept distinct from source-repeat variability. These choices mean that the reported quantities answer a bounded transfer question rather than a deployment question in which the source must discover the feature universe without any target-independent registry.

Known-truth stress tests and repeated-participant controls are method-validation evidence, not biological validation. The collapse from naive to participant-grouped AUROC demonstrates why repeated samples can inflate internal estimates, while null and batch-only controls show that the audit pipeline can remain near chance when no disease signal is planted. The origin-held-out PRISM analysis provides the available biological generalization test within the current package, but two public cohorts do not establish clinical generalization across geography, protocol, disease activity or treatment. The binary endpoint also combines Crohn disease, ulcerative colitis and heterogeneous control definitions. Those limitations are part of the estimand and should be reported rather than hidden by a more complex model.

The residual novelty is therefore methodological and evidentiary. Cross-study microbiome evaluation, compositional analysis, graph learning and confound adjustment each have established precedents [10–35]. The contribution is their integration into a single reproducible contract in which every representation comparison is directional, every label-dependent step is source-bounded, and every graph result is accompanied by validity, topology and capacity gates. This framing is narrower than a new GAT architecture but stronger against the specific failure modes identified by the reviewers. It also makes the paper reusable: the same manifest, split, bootstrap and source-registry design can audit another disease or a future third cohort without changing the estimand.

Several limitations remain. The exact-name metabolite intersection was empty, so the present cross-cohort evidence is microbiome-only. The capacity comparator is approximate and should be accompanied by parameter-count and optimizer records in the release package. PRISM origin labels identify discovery and validation strata but do not by themselves identify every sequencing, geographic or treatment confounder. The candidate graph features were not experimentally validated. Finally, the ten-split summaries average split-specific estimates and interval endpoints; they are not pooled cohort-level confidence intervals. These constraints delimit the claims and identify the next useful experiments: a third independent cohort, nested source-side tuning, compositional association alternatives and prospective validation of candidate features.

The framework is thus best viewed as a reporting and evaluation standard for portability claims. It supports a conditional relational gain in one transfer direction and a clear negative result in others. Both outcomes are scientifically informative because the predeclared contract makes them comparable.
