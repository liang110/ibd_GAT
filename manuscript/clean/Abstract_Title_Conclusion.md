# Abstract

Microbiome-based classification of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is often assessed under split and representation choices that alter the estimand. We developed an estimand-explicit, source-only protocol for bidirectional cross-cohort evaluation of relational and tabular microbiome representations. In HMP2/IBDMDB (1,626 included observations from 130 participants) and PRISM (220 observations from 220 operational participants), a 126-feature microbiome contract was fixed before fitting. A repeated-participant control with 80 synthetic participants showed a mean sample-level participant overlap of 43 and zero overlap under participant grouping; current-contract AUROCs were 0.960 and 0.552182, respectively. Across ten source fits, HMP2-to-PRISM No-Prior graph attention exceeded elastic net, random forest and a no-message-passing multilayer perceptron by common-draw AUROC differences of 0.1425, 0.0991 and 0.0897. Reverse transfer favoured tabular references. A same-architecture topology intervention produced a positive forward real-versus-rewired contrast, but only 5/10 source splits were positive; identity-aware sensitivity was positive forward and near zero in reverse. Direct PRISM origin-stratum transfer also favoured tabular references, and reverse participant-level Brier skill relative to a source-prevalence constant was −0.0003. The contribution is a reproducible decision protocol that separates model ranking, conditional topology effects, provenance sensitivity and probability error, rather than a universal graph-model claim.

Keywords: inflammatory bowel disease; microbiome; directional transfer; graph attention; compositional data; external validation; reproducibility

# Conclusion

Under the frozen 126-feature contract and source-only participant-aware evaluation, the relational representation contributed additional information in HMP2-to-PRISM transfer, including against a capacity-matched no-message-passing MLP, whereas the reverse direction and PRISM origin transfer did not reproduce that advantage. The framework makes this conditional result auditable through source-bounded fitting, known-truth controls, topology diagnostics, participant-cluster bootstrap and a unified prediction ledger. The evidence supports a directional framework claim for the declared HMP2/PRISM IBD-versus-control endpoint; it does not establish universal GAT superiority, causal microbial interactions, geographic effects, clinical utility or shared-metabolome transfer.
