# Title

An auditable framework for directional transferability of relational and tabular microbiome representations in inflammatory bowel disease

# Abstract

External validation is the point at which an IBD microbiome classifier must distinguish disease-associated signal from measurement scale, repeated participants, clinical leakage, cohort origin and representation-specific structure. We present an auditable framework that makes these boundaries explicit before model comparison. The framework freezes a shared microbiome contract, fits every transformation and label-dependent selection step on source-training participants, preserves participant units, evaluates both transfer directions and separates method-validation controls from biological transfer. We applied it to HMP2/IBDMDB and PRISM, where the harmonized contract contained 126 shared microbial features and no exact-name shared metabolites. Participant grouping reduced a repeated-sample AUROC from 0.945 under naive splitting to 0.464 under grouped evaluation. In the primary relational comparison, a No-Prior Spearman graph-attention model reached AUROC 0.846 for HMP2-to-PRISM transfer, compared with 0.715 for elastic net and 0.768 for random forest; the reverse direction favoured the tabular references (0.612 versus 0.711 and 0.680). PRISM origin-held-out transfer likewise showed no graph advantage. Topology nulls, edge-set overlap, a capacity-matched no-message comparator and participant-cluster paired bootstrap localized the result to a conditional, direction-specific representation gain. The contribution is therefore an evidence-standard and reusable transfer framework, with a worked IBD application, rather than a universally superior GAT or a validated microbiome biomarker panel.

# Conclusion

The reconstructed study defines portability as a directional, contract-specific estimand. A frozen feature universe, source-only fitting, participant-aware evaluation, known-truth controls, origin-held-out transfer, topology diagnostics, capacity control and paired uncertainty together expose when a relational representation adds information and when it does not. The IBD application shows a forward HMP2-to-PRISM gain for the No-Prior Spearman graph, but no symmetric or origin-independent advantage. This bounded result answers the reviewers’ central concern: the framework is useful because it makes leakage, cohort origin and topology auditable, not because GATv2 is assumed to be universally portable. Biological interpretation remains hypothesis-generating until the candidate features are independently validated.
